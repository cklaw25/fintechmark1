Beginner's guide

The three tracks

Open the one you want. We chose Fraud & Security.

Payments: where payments stall or fail

A payment is a parcel handed between couriers: your bank → a network → their bank. Each handover can lose, delay or return it.

Day to day: ops teams chase "where's my money?" tickets, investigate returned transfers and handle chargebacks. Finance teams reconcile (match incoming payments to invoices), often by hand in Excel.

  • International transfers take 1–5 days and cost about 6% on average worldwide (World Bank).
  • Returned payments come back with cryptic codes (AC04, RR04) and no next step.
  • Small businesses wait 60–90 days on invoices.
  • "Who paid me £340 with reference J SMITH?"
Access to Finance: people and small businesses turned away

A lender is a bouncer checking ID. Your ID is your credit file. No file, or a messy one, means no entry, even if you're trustworthy.

Day to day: underwriters review statements and scores and price the loan to its risk. Credit agencies (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion) keep the files. Borrowers spend weeks gathering paperwork.

  • Millions of UK adults have thin or no credit files: migrants, students, people who only use cash.
  • Rejections arrive without reasons, so applicants can't fix anything.
  • Checking a £10k loan costs nearly as much as a £1M one, so small loans get ignored.
  • What changed: Open Banking lets people share real bank data with consent, and AI can read messy statements.
Fraud & Security: scams that look convincing (our track)

Criminals no longer break into the vault. They talk the customer into opening it.

Day to day: fraud analysts review flagged payments, call customers ("did you make this?") and handle victim claims. Identity teams run KYC ("know your customer") checks on new accounts.

  • Authorised push payment (APP) fraud: the victim is tricked into sending money themselves. Fake landlord, fake "bank security team", romance and crypto investment scams. UK losses run to hundreds of millions of pounds a year.
  • Since Oct 2024, UK banks must reimburse most APP victims (up to £85k), so every scam they miss costs them directly. That makes banks the buyer.
  • AI makes scams better: cloned voices, deepfake calls, flawless phishing, fake screenshots in seconds.
  • The hard part: the victim wants to pay, so normal security checks all pass.
Fintech hack vs AI hack
  • Money moves can't be undone. Show what happens when the AI is wrong. We advise and pause, never auto-block.
  • "The AI said no" isn't allowed. Show why: highlight the evidence.
  • Data is sensitive. Ask for consent, collect the minimum, never ask for bank logins.
  • Name the person and the pound amount. "Margaret nearly lost £8,000" beats "users face friction".
  • Say who pays. Bank, marketplace, accounting tool, university.
Fraud & Security shortlist

Seven ideas

Why now, for all of them: AI makes scams cheap and convincing, and banks now pay for every scam they miss. Trust, for all of them: we never move or block money; we pause and explain, and every red flag points to where we found it.

F1 · Pause

Recommended

A scam check before you pay.

Person
Margaret, 68. "Barclays fraud team" tells her to move £8,000 to a safe account.
Flow
Paste the message or answer 3 questions. Get a red, amber or green verdict, with the scam phrases highlighted in her own words and one safe next step: "Hang up, call the number on your card."
Who pays
Banks, built into the payment screen.
Build
Easy one text box and a structured AI verdict.

F3 · Invoice Guard

Most original · Zaro fit

Stops "we changed our bank details" invoice scams.

Person
Sam, runs a 10-person building firm. A "supplier" asks him to pay a £14k invoice to new bank details.
Flow
Upload the email or invoice. It's compared with the supplier's past invoices for changed bank details, lookalike domains and urgency, and gets a mocked bank name check. "Call the supplier on the number you already have."
Who pays
Xero or QuickBooks add-on, business banks.
Build
Medium needs sample invoice history.

F2 · Rent Check

Most relatable

Rental deposit scam checker.

Person
Wei, new international LSE student. An "overseas landlord" wants £2,000 before any viewing.
Flow
Paste the listing and messages. It checks price against the area, scam-script red flags and reused photos, plus a mocked Land Registry owner check. Get a verdict and questions to ask the landlord.
Who pays
Universities, SpareRoom or Rightmove, banks.
Build
Easy same engine as Pause.

F4 · Paid?

Fotor fit

Fake payment-screenshot checker.

Person
Priya sells on Vinted. A buyer sends a "transfer sent" screenshot; she ships and the money never arrives.
Flow
Upload the screenshot. AI reads the amount, reference and time, spots signs of editing and matches it against her real incoming payments: "Not in your account. Don't ship."
Who pays
Marketplaces, seller banks.
Build
Medium needs vision AI and a fake test set (made with Fotor).

F5 · Mule Alert

Spots job ads that recruit money mules.

Person
Jay, 19. Instagram ad: "Earn £500/week, just receive money into your account."
Flow
Paste or screenshot the ad. It flags the recruitment patterns and explains the cost: criminal record, account closed, a fraud marker on file.
Who pays
Banks, universities, social platforms.
Build
Easy

F6 · Is It Really Mum?

Checks voice-clone "family emergency" calls.

Person
A dad gets a call in his daughter's cloned voice: "Lost my phone, need £600 now."
Flow
One tap sends a challenge to her real number or asks for the family safe word. The AI flags the script.
Build
Hard to demo

F7 · Second Pair of Eyes

A trusted contact confirms unusual payments.

Person
An older person sends an unusually large payment to a new payee late at night.
Flow
A chosen family member gets pinged to confirm, with the AI's summary of why it looks odd.
Build
Medium needs mocked bank data.
